When you describe a piece of music, you need a set of headings that works for any music at all, from a chant to a film score to a song. The elements of music are those headings. There are seven: duration, pitch, tone colour, texture, structure, dynamics and articulation. The listening template in this app uses them as its headings.

:::def Elements of music :: the seven aspects of sound that any piece can be described under: duration, pitch, tone colour, texture, structure, dynamics and articulation.

## Duration

Duration covers everything to do with time: rhythm, beat, metre, tempo and how notes are grouped. When you write about duration, say what the metre is, how fast the beat goes, whether the rhythm is simple or syncopated, and whether there is a rhythmic pattern that stands out.

:::def Syncopation :: rhythm that stresses notes between the beats or on weak beats, pulling against the regular pulse.

:::clip A1_Beethoven_Sym5_opening :: Beethoven, Symphony No. 5, opening :: Listen for the short-short-short-long rhythm. It is the main idea of the whole movement.

:::clip A1_Joplin_MapleLeafRag :: Joplin, Maple Leaf Rag :: Syncopation: the left hand keeps the beat and the right hand lands between the beats.

## Pitch

Pitch is how high or low the notes are. It has three parts. Melody is the line of single notes you can sing, described by its contour, range and whether it moves by step or leap. Harmony is the chords under or around the melody, and how they change. Tonality is the key: major, minor, pentatonic, or something else. Earlier lessons covered each of these in detail. In a listening response, write a line on each.

:::def Tonality :: the key and scale a piece is built on: major, minor, pentatonic, or another scale, or no clear key at all (atonal).

:::clip A1_Tchaikovsky_SwanLake :: Tchaikovsky, Swan Lake, Act 2 opening :: An oboe melody in a minor key, over harp and shivering strings.

## Tone colour

Tone colour is the quality of a sound that tells you which instrument or voice is making it. A flute and a trumpet can play the same pitch at the same loudness and still sound nothing alike. The word timbre means the same thing. To describe tone colour, name the instruments or voices, say how they are played (bowed, plucked, muted, with vibrato), and choose words for the quality: bright, dark, warm, thin, harsh, mellow, breathy, nasal.

:::def Tone colour (timbre) :: the quality of a sound that distinguishes one instrument or voice from another playing the same note.

:::example tone-colours

:::clip A1_Vivaldi_OboeConcerto :: Vivaldi, Oboe Concerto in C, Larghetto :: Your instrument's tone colour in a slow melody.

:::clip A1_Grieg_MorningMood :: Grieg, Morning Mood :: Flute and oboe taking turns with the melody.

:::clip A1_SaintSaens_Aquarium :: Saint-Saëns, Aquarium :: Glassy keyboard, flute and muted strings in a high register.

:::clip A1_Mozart_QueenOfTheNight :: Mozart, Der Hölle Rache :: A soprano at the top of her range.

:::clip A1_Tchaikovsky_SugarPlum :: Tchaikovsky, Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy :: Celesta and bass clarinet.

:::clip A1_Gershwin_RhapsodyInBlue :: Gershwin, Rhapsody in Blue, opening :: Clarinet, with the opening glissando.

## Texture

Texture is how many layers of sound there are and how they relate. One melody alone is monophonic. A melody with chords or an accompaniment under it is homophonic. Two or more independent melodies at once are polyphonic. The next lesson deals with texture on its own.

:::def Texture :: the number of layers in the music and the way they combine: one line, melody with accompaniment, or several independent lines.

## Structure

Structure is how the piece is organised in time: its sections, how they repeat and contrast, and the overall plan, such as verse and chorus, or A B A. Lesson A4 covers the common plans. In a response, name the sections with letters or words and give the times at which they change.

:::def Structure (form) :: the plan of a piece's sections, usually written with letters: A B A, A A B, and so on.

## Dynamics

Dynamics are the levels of loudness and the changes between them. Say how loud the music is overall, whether it changes suddenly or gradually, and where the loudest moment comes.

:::clip A1_RStrauss_Zarathustra :: Richard Strauss, Also sprach Zarathustra, opening :: One long crescendo from almost nothing to the full orchestra.

## Articulation

Articulation is how notes are attacked and connected: smooth (legato), detached (staccato), accented, or with slides and bends. Lesson L13 covered the markings. In a response, describe the articulation of the main melody and anything that stands out, such as sharp accents in the brass.

## Using the elements

A good description does not need every element in every sentence. It picks the elements that matter most in the passage and says something exact about each: not "the dynamics change" but "a crescendo from piano to fortissimo over about eight bars, reached at 0:42". Timings and musical terms turn an opinion into evidence. The listening template in My works has one line for each element so nothing is forgotten.

:::aside Quick check :: If a question asks you to describe a passage, work through the seven elements in order and write one precise sentence for each that applies. Then say which two or three matter most for the effect of the passage.

:::quiz
