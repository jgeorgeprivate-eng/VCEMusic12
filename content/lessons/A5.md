Listening and responding means hearing a piece or an excerpt, identifying what the music does, and writing or saying it in musical terms with evidence. This lesson gives you a method and a template. The template is the same one you fill in for each of your own works in My works.

:::def Excerpt :: a short passage taken from a longer piece, usually between thirty seconds and two minutes.
:::def Musical evidence :: a specific, checkable observation about the music, with a term and usually a time: "a crescendo to fortissimo at 0:42".

## The listening template

The template has four parts. First, the seven elements, one line each: duration, pitch (melody, harmony, tonality), tone colour, texture, structure, dynamics and articulation. Second, compositional devices: what the main idea is and what happens to it. Third, the approach to organisation: the form, with sections and timings. Fourth, the effect: what mood, image or character the music creates and which choices create it. For a work you are studying, add the context: who wrote or performed it, when, where and for what.

:::def Listening template :: a form with a line for each element of music, the devices, the organisation, the effect and the context, used to record what you hear in a work.

## A method for the first hearing

Before the music starts, read the question or decide what you are listening for. During the first hearing, do not write much. Tap the beat, notice the instruments, and mark the time when something changes. After it ends, write one line for each element from memory. On the second hearing, check and add timings. On the third, fill the gaps and listen for the devices.

## Identifying characteristics

A characteristic is a feature you can name. The strongest answers go from general to specific. "The texture is melody and accompaniment" is general. "The texture is melody and accompaniment: a solo oboe over sustained string chords, thinning to the oboe alone at 1:10" is specific. For each element, aim for the term, then the instruments or notes involved, then the time.

## Writing a short response

A short response is usually one paragraph per element the question asks about, or one paragraph that covers the two or three elements that matter most. Each paragraph makes a claim and backs it with evidence. Use the terms from these lessons. Avoid words that only describe your reaction, such as nice or boring, unless you then explain what in the music causes it.

Here is a model response on the opening of a piece you can hear below. "The excerpt is in a slow 4/4 with a gentle, steady repeated note in the middle register running through the whole passage (duration). The melody above it moves mostly by step in a major key, with a range of about an octave (pitch). It is for solo piano, with a soft, warm tone colour (tone colour). The texture is melody and accompaniment: the tune in the right hand, the repeated note and quiet chords beneath (texture). The dynamics stay around piano with small swells at the ends of phrases (dynamics), and the articulation is legato throughout (articulation). The opening section returns after a louder, minor-key middle section, so the form is ternary (structure)."

## Practising

Use the template on any music you like, not only set works. Three short responses a week, on three different styles, build the habit faster than one long one. My works keeps every template you complete and turns it into a revision card, so the work you do now becomes your revision later.

:::aside Quick check :: Every sentence in a response should contain at least one musical term and, where possible, one time. If a sentence has neither, it is probably an opinion rather than evidence.

:::quiz
