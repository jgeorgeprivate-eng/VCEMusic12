There is a second question to ask of every piece: not only how is it organised, but what effect does it create and how. Effect means the mood, image, character or feeling the music gives a listener. Composers and performers create effect by choices in the elements of music. Once you can connect a choice to its effect, you can describe any piece, and you can make those choices in your own music and playing.

:::def Effect :: the mood, image, character or feeling a passage of music creates in the listener.

## What each element can do

Duration: a fast tempo creates energy, excitement or panic; a slow one calm, grief or grandeur. A steady beat feels secure; syncopation feels restless; a gallop rhythm suggests motion. Pitch: a high register is light, bright or tense; a low one dark, heavy or threatening. A major key sounds open and content; a minor key sad, serious or sinister. Wide leaps feel dramatic; steps feel smooth. Tone colour: strings warm and singing; brass heroic or menacing; flute cool and airy; oboe plaintive; percussion driving or military; the human voice intimate. Texture: a single line is lonely or pure; thick layers are powerful or chaotic; a texture that builds creates expectation. Dynamics: soft is intimate, secret or distant; loud is grand, angry or joyful; a crescendo builds tension; a sudden change shocks. Articulation: staccato is playful, nervous or mechanical; legato is calm, flowing or tender; accents are forceful.

These are common associations, not fixed meanings. Composers use them, combine them and sometimes play against them, which is why the next point matters.

No single element creates an effect alone. A fast tempo in a minor key with low brass and accents is menacing; the same tempo in a major key with high flutes and staccato is playful. When you describe effect, name at least two elements and say how they work together.

## Pictures in music

Music that sets out to describe a scene, a story or a picture is called programme music. Composers do it by imitating sounds (birdsong, a storm, a gallop) and by using the associations above.

:::def Programme music :: instrumental music that tells a story or paints a picture, often with a title that says what.

:::clip A6_SaintSaens_Aquarium :: Saint-Saëns, Aquarium :: Water from high register, tremolo and rippling runs.

:::clip A6_Vivaldi_Spring :: Vivaldi, Spring :: Birdsong, a stream and a storm.

:::clip A6_RimskyKorsakov_Bumblebee :: Rimsky-Korsakov, Flight of the Bumblebee :: An insect from fast chromatic notes.

:::clip A6_Grieg_MorningMood :: Grieg, Morning Mood :: A sunrise from a climbing melody and a growing orchestra.

:::clip A6_Chopin_Raindrop :: Chopin, Raindrop Prelude :: Rain from a repeated note; a storm from the minor middle section.

## Tension and drama

Tension is made by raising something steadily and withholding the release: speeding up, getting louder, climbing higher, adding layers, or repeating a phrase more and more insistently. The release is the arrival: a loud final chord, a return to the tonic, a sudden silence.

:::clip A6_Grieg_MountainKing :: Grieg, In the Hall of the Mountain King :: One tune, faster and louder each time, until it bursts.

:::clip A6_Wagner_Valkyries :: Wagner, Ride of the Valkyries :: Drama from a leaping brass melody over rushing strings.

:::clip A6_Rossini_WilliamTell :: Rossini, William Tell Overture, Finale :: Excitement from a galloping rhythm that never stops.

## Calm and cold

Calm comes from soft dynamics, slow or flexible tempo, legato articulation, a thin or gentle texture and harmony that does not push anywhere. Cold and bleakness come from bare, wide intervals, high thin tone colours, slow tempo and the absence of a warm melody.

:::clip A6_Debussy_ClairDeLune :: Debussy, Clair de lune :: Calm from soft dynamics, rubato and blurred harmony.

:::clip A6_VaughanWilliams_Antartica :: Vaughan Williams, Sinfonia antartica, Prelude :: Cold from wide intervals, wind machine and wordless voices.

:::clip A6_Westlake_Antarctica :: Westlake, Antarctica :: An Australian view of the same place.

## Writing about effect

Name the effect in one or two words, then explain it with elements and evidence: "The passage sounds menacing (effect) because of the low register of the brass melody, the minor key and the accented, repeated rhythm in the timpani (elements), which build in a crescendo from 0:30 to the fortissimo at 0:55 (evidence)." Effect first, then cause, then where.

:::aside Quick check :: For any effect you name, find at least two elements that produce it and one place in the music where you can hear them doing so.
