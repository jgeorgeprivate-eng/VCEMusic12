Harmonising a melody means choosing the chords that go under it. With only the three primary chords, I, IV and V, you can harmonise almost any melody in a major key, because between them those three chords contain every note of the scale. The skill is choosing the chord that contains the melody's important notes in each bar, and ending in a way that sounds finished.

:::def Harmonise :: to add chords to a melody so that each chord fits the melody notes above it.
:::def Chord tone :: a note that belongs to the chord sounding at that moment. Other notes are non-chord tones.

## Which chord fits

Look at the notes on the strong beats of a bar, beats 1 and 3 in 4/4. Then ask which of the three chords contains them. In C major, I is C E G, IV is F A C and V is G B D. If the strong beats are C and E, chord I fits. If they are F and A, chord IV fits. If they are G and B, chord V fits. Notes on weak beats can be outside the chord. They are often passing notes or neighbour notes that move between chord tones.

:::def Passing note :: a note on a weak beat that steps between two chord tones and does not belong to the chord.

:::example triads key=C caption="The three primary chords in C major, with ii and vi for comparison. For this lesson, use only I, IV and V."

Some notes belong to two chords. C is in both I and IV; G is in both I and V. When a bar could take either, choose by ear, and remember two habits that work well when you are starting out: begin on I, and end with V then I.

## The cadence at the end

An 8-bar melody usually ends on the tonic, and when you start harmonising it's often a good idea to make the last two chords V then I. That perfect cadence makes the ending sound finished. Later, when you are more confident, you can choose different harmonisations to achieve particular effects. For now, you should stick with ending on V then I. If the melody ends on the tonic but the last chord is IV, the ending sounds soft and incomplete. If the last chord is V, the tonic in the melody clashes with it.

:::example progression key=C id=V-I caption="V to I: the ending every harmonised melody wants."

## Harmoniser

The app writes an 8-bar melody. Choose I, IV or V for each bar, play the result and check. In this tool, and likely in the music subject you are doing, a chord counts as fitting the melody when the notes on beats 1 and 3 are in that chord. Try all three keys.

:::tool harmoniser

## Writing out your harmonisation

When you harmonise a melody for your folio, write the chord symbols above the staff (C, F, G in C major, or the Roman numerals I, IV, V) and play the chords as block chords, broken chords or a bass line under the tune. The next two lessons cover writing a melody over chords and building a bass line.

:::aside Quick check :: If a bar sounds wrong, find the note on beat 1 and ask which of I, IV and V contains it. Then check beat 3 the same way. If no single chord contains both, the bar may need two chords, one per half.

:::quiz
