Most melodies are written over a chord progression. The chords give the melody a skeleton: on the strong beats, the melody usually uses notes of the chord, and between them it moves through the other notes of the scale. This lesson gives you a three-step method and a writer to try it in.

:::def Chord progression :: the sequence of chords a melody is written over, such as I – IV – V – I.
:::def Phrase :: a musical sentence, usually two or four bars, ending at a point of rest.

## Step one: chord notes on the strong beats

Choose a key and a progression. In each bar, place a chord tone on beat 1 and usually on beat 3. In C major with I – IV – V – I, that means notes from C E G, then F A C, then G B D, then C E G. A melody made only of chord tones already fits the chords, though it sounds plain.

:::example melody notes="[C] E4h G4h | [F] F4h A4h | [G] G4h B4h | [C] C5w" key=C time=4/4 caption="Chord tones only on every beat. Correct, but it moves in leaps and has no flow."

## Step two: scale notes between them

Now join the chord tones with notes of the scale on the weak beats. These passing notes and neighbour notes make the melody move by step, which is easier to sing and sounds more like a tune. A neighbour note steps away from a chord tone and back again.

:::def Neighbour note :: a note a step above or below a chord tone that returns to it.

:::example melody notes="[C] E4q F4q G4q A4q | [F] F4q G4q A4q G4q | [G] G4q A4q B4q D5q | [C] C5w" key=C time=4/4 caption="The same chord tones with scale notes between them. Now the melody moves by step."

## Step three: rhythm, articulation and dynamics

Vary the rhythm so the phrase has a shape: a longer note at the end of each phrase, some quavers where the line moves. Then decide how it should be played. For example: slurs for a smooth line, staccato for a light one, an accent on the high point, a crescendo toward it and a diminuendo after. Write these on the score.

:::example melody notes="[C] ( E4q F4e G4e A4q ) G4q | [F] {p} < F4q. G4e A4q G4q | [G] G4e A4e B4q D5q ! {f} C5q' | [C] {mf} C5h. rq" key=C time=4/4 caption="With rhythm, a slur, a crescendo to the high note and a staccato on the last beat of bar 3."

## Shaping a phrase

Four-bar phrases that are musically satisfying often have one high point, typically about two thirds of the way through, and often end on a chord tone with a longer note. The second phrase of an eight-bar melody often starts like the first and ends differently, lower and more final. That is repetition and variation at the smallest scale.

## Melody and bass writer

Choose a key, a progression, 4/4 or 3/4, and four or eight bars. Pick a note length, then tap keys to enter the melody one note at a time; the staff fills in and the chord for the current bar is named. The Chord notes button lights the keys that belong to the current chord. Add a bass line from the menu, play it with chords, save it, and export it as an image or a MIDI file for your school software.

:::tool melody-writer

:::aside Quick check :: Play your melody without the chords. If it still sounds like a tune, the shape is right. Then play it with the chords. If a strong-beat note clashes, move it to the nearest chord tone.

:::quiz
