The bass is the lowest part, and it does two jobs at once: it tells the ear which chord is sounding, and it gives the music its rhythmic drive. A bass line built from the roots of the chords is the simplest accompaniment there is, and it works in many styles, from hymns to rock. This lesson gives you four patterns to use under any progression.

:::def Bass line :: the lowest part of the texture, usually built from the roots of the chords.
:::def Root :: the note a chord is named after and built on.

## Root notes

The plainest bass plays the root of each chord, one note per bar or per chord change. It is the backbone of the progression. Listen for how the roots of I – IV – V – I rise to IV, step up to V and fall back to I.

:::example progression key=C id=I-IV-V-I caption="I – IV – V – I in C: the roots are C, F, G, C. Tap to hear the chords; the progression player in L11 adds a bass note."

## Root and fifth

Alternating the root and the fifth of the chord gives the bass a swing. In country and folk music the bass alternates root and fifth under the chords (oom-pah). In a waltz the bass plays the root on beat 1 and the chord answers on beats 2 and 3 (oom-pah-pah). The fifth is a perfect 5th above the root; in a root-position triad it is the top note.

:::example melody notes="C4h G4h | F4h C5h | G4h D5h | C4w" key=C time=4/4 transpose=-24 caption="Root and fifth under I – IV – V – I. Written here in the treble clef; it sounds two octaves lower, where a bass plays." voice=strings

## Broken chords

Playing the notes of the chord one after another, for example root, third, fifth, third, makes a flowing accompaniment, like the arpeggio patterns of many ballads. The Alberti bass of classical piano music is one version of this, played in the order root, fifth, third, fifth.

:::def Broken chord (arpeggio) :: the notes of a chord played one after another instead of together.

:::example melody notes="C4q E4q G4q E4q | F4q A4q C5q A4q | G4q B4q D5q B4q | C4w" key=C time=4/4 transpose=-24 caption="Broken chords under I – IV – V – I, sounding two octaves lower than written." voice=piano

## Walking bass

A walking bass plays a note on every beat, moving mostly by step toward the root of the next chord. It is the sound of jazz and blues. The notes on beat 1 are usually roots; the notes between can be other chord tones or scale notes that lead to the next root.

:::example melody notes="C4q E4q G4q E4q | F4q G4q A4q F4q | G4q A4q B4q D5q | C4w" key=C time=4/4 transpose=-24 caption="A walking bass: roots on beat 1, stepping toward the next root. Sounds two octaves lower than written." voice=strings

## Choosing a bass pattern

Match the pattern to the style and the effect. Roots alone tend to sound calm and simple, root and fifth bouncy and danceable, broken chords flowing and gentle, and walking bass lively and jazzy. Tempo and rhythm change the effect too, so try each pattern at different speeds. In the melody writer, the Bass menu adds any of the four under your melody, and the MIDI export includes the bass as its own track.

:::tool melody-writer

:::aside Quick check :: For now, make the bass note on beat 1 the root of that bar's chord, and if the chord changes mid-bar, change the bass with it. Composers often put the third or the fifth in the bass instead (an inversion) to make the bass line smoother. Keep to roots until the four patterns are secure.

:::quiz
