A short idea is enough to make a whole phrase, and a phrase is enough to make a piece, if you know how to develop it. The techniques are the ones you analysed in lesson A2: repetition, sequence, augmentation, diminution, inversion, fragmentation and contrast. This lesson turns them into tools you use on your own motif.

:::def Motif :: a short musical idea, three to six notes, that you can recognise whenever it returns.
:::def Development :: making new music from a motif by repeating and changing it.

## Start small

A good motif has a clear rhythm and a clear shape. Three or four notes with one distinctive feature, a leap, a dotted rhythm, a repeated note, are easier to recognise and develop than a long tune. Beethoven's four notes are the famous case.

:::example motif-devices

## A plan for a four-bar phrase

A reliable plan for a four-bar phrase: state the motif; repeat it in sequence a step higher or lower; fragment it or augment it; then end with something that contrasts and comes to rest on the tonic. Listen for the balance between what stays the same and what changes. Too much repetition is dull; too much change and the listener loses the thread. This is one plan of many. Once it works for you, try changing the order of the devices.

## Motif developer

Tap three to five notes to make a motif. Then apply devices one after another to build a phrase, and play it. Try the same motif with different sequences of devices and notice which phrase sounds most like one idea growing.

:::tool motif-developer

## Writing it down

When you have a phrase you like, write it in the melody writer over a progression so it has chords and a bass, and save it. In your folio, name the devices you used and where: "Bars 1 and 2 are the motif and its sequence a step lower; bar 3 is the first three notes fragmented and repeated; bar 4 is a contrasting cadence." That sentence is the documentation lesson C7 asks for.

:::aside Quick check :: After each device, sing the result back. If you can still hear the original motif in it, the device has worked. If you cannot, you have written contrast, which is fine in small doses. Once or twice in a phrase is usually enough.

:::quiz
