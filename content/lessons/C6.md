In Unit 2 the creating task changes direction. Instead of asking how a piece is organised, it asks what effect it creates and how. You choose an effect, a mood, an image, a character, and then make the choices in each element of music that produce it. Lesson A6 showed how composers do this; this lesson has you do it yourself.

:::def Intended effect :: the mood, image or character you decide a piece should create before you write it.

## Decide the effect first

Write the effect down in one or two words before you touch a note: calm, menacing, playful, grand, cold, rushing. Everything else follows from it. A vague aim ("nice") gives you nothing to choose with; a precise one tells you the tempo, register, dynamics, articulation and instrument almost on its own.

## The effect sandbox

Take any melody, including your own saved pieces, and change its tempo, register, dynamics, articulation, instrument, accompaniment and tonality. Play it after each change and notice what the change does. Then set every control to produce one effect and press Describe to get a list of your settings in words.

:::tool effect-sandbox

## Choosing by element

For calm: slow, middle or low register, soft, legato, a warm instrument, sustained chords or a drone, major or gently minor. For menace: moderate tempo, low register, growing dynamics, accents, low strings or brass, a drone or pulsing chords, minor. For playfulness: fast, high, moderate dynamics, staccato, flute or piano, no accompaniment or light pulsing chords, major. For grandeur: moderate tempo, full register, loud, marcato, brass or strings, sustained chords, major. These are starting points, not rules; the sandbox lets you test any combination.

## Any sound source

The task allows any sound: voices, instruments, found sounds, electronic sounds or a phone app. The same choices apply to all of them. A recording of rain under a slow flute line is as much a composition for effect as a string quartet, and a graphic score may document it better than staff notation (lesson L12).

## Writing it up

State the effect, then the choices, then the evidence: "Intended effect: cold and bleak. Choices: slow tempo (60), high register, pp with no crescendo, legato, flute sound over a bare fifth drone, minor. The drone and the empty high line do most of the work." The sandbox's Describe button gives you the middle sentence.

:::aside Quick check :: Ask someone to listen without being told the effect, and to name it. If they say something close, the choices are working. If not, change the two elements that matter most for that effect, usually tempo and tonality, before anything else.

:::quiz
