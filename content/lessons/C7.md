Your folio needs more than the music. It needs a record of how the music was made and why it is the way it is. Documenting means keeping the music in a form others can read or hear: notation, a lead sheet, a rhythm grid, a graphic score or a recording. Reflecting means explaining your choices in musical terms, what you changed, and what you would do next.

:::def Documenting :: keeping a record of a piece in notation, another written form or a recording, so it can be read, played or heard again.
:::def Reflecting :: explaining in musical terms what you set out to do, what you did and how well it worked.

## What to document

Keep the music itself: export the notation as an image or a MIDI file from the melody writer, or record it in the practice room. Keep the process too: the starting motif, a version from halfway through, and the final version. Date each one. A folio that shows the piece changing is worth more than one that shows only the result.

## How to reflect

Answer six questions. What did I set out to make, and for what effect or organisation? What was my starting idea? Which elements did I shape, and how? Which compositional devices did I use, and where? What did I change along the way, and why? Does it do what I intended, and what would I change next time? Each answer should use the terms from the lessons: the names of the elements, the devices, the chords and the form.

## Reflection form

Choose one of your saved pieces. The form asks the six questions and saves the answers with the piece. Copy as text puts the whole reflection on the clipboard for your folio document.

:::tool reflection

## Model reflection

"I set out to write a calm four-bar phrase in G major over I – IV – V – I (intention). The starting idea was a three-note motif, G A B, in crotchets (material). I kept the tempo slow, the register in the middle of the oboe, the dynamic piano, and the articulation legato, with sustained string chords underneath (elements). Bar 2 is the motif in sequence a step higher; bar 3 fragments it to two notes repeated; bar 4 is a contrasting cadence on D to G (devices). I first ended on a crotchet, but it sounded cut off, so I changed it to a dotted minim (change). It sounds calm, but the high note in bar 3 is a little abrupt; next time I would approach it by step (result)."

:::aside Quick check :: Every sentence of a reflection should contain a musical term. If a sentence could be about any piece of music, or any piece of schoolwork, it needs rewriting.

:::quiz
