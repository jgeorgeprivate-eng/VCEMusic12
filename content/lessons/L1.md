Written music is a set of instructions for sound. Each note on the page tells you two things: which pitch to play and how long to hold it. This lesson covers pitch, which is how high or low a note is. The next lesson covers how long notes last.

:::def Pitch :: how high or low a sound is. A high pitch has a faster vibration than a low one.
:::def Staff :: the set of five horizontal lines that notes are written on. Notes sit on the lines or in the spaces between them.

## The staff and the treble clef

The five lines of the staff and the four spaces between them each stand for a different pitch. Higher on the staff means higher in pitch. The symbol at the start of every line of music is a clef, and it fixes which pitch each line stands for. Oboe music uses the treble clef. Its curl wraps around the second line from the bottom, which is why it is also called the G clef: that line is G.

:::def Clef :: the symbol at the start of a staff that fixes the pitch of each line. The treble clef sets the second line from the bottom as G above middle C.

With the treble clef, the lines from the bottom up are E, G, B, D and F. The spaces from the bottom up are F, A, C and E, which spells the word FACE. Tap either example to hear the notes.

:::example staff-names which=spaces

Note names go in a repeating cycle of seven letters, A B C D E F G, and then start again at A. Moving up one line or space moves one letter up the alphabet. So the pattern on the staff from the bottom line is E F G A B C D E F.

## Middle C and ledger lines

The staff only has nine positions, and music often goes higher or lower than that. For notes outside the staff, short extra lines called ledger lines are drawn through or beside the note. Middle C, the C near the middle of a piano keyboard, sits on the first ledger line below the treble staff.

:::def Ledger line :: a short line drawn above or below the staff so a note can be written outside the five lines.
:::def Middle C :: the C closest to the middle of a piano. It is written on the first ledger line below the treble staff.

:::example ledger

Count ledger lines the same way as staff lines: each line or space is one letter. The note just below the staff, hanging under the bottom line, is D. The first ledger line below is C, the space below that is B, and the second ledger line is A. Above the staff, the space above the top line is G, the first ledger line is A, and so on.

## Octaves and note names

Because the letters repeat, there are several notes called C. Each one is an octave above the last. To say exactly which C you mean, musicians add a number: middle C is C4, the C on the third space of the treble staff is C5, and the C on the second ledger line above the staff is C6. The number goes up by one each time you pass a C going upward. In this app, note names with a number always use this system.

:::def Octave :: the distance between one note and the next note with the same letter name. The higher note vibrates twice as fast.

## Sharps, flats and naturals

The seven letter names cover the white keys of a piano. The black keys sit between most of them. A sharp sign raises a note by one semitone, to the next key up, and a flat sign lowers it by one semitone, to the next key down. A natural sign cancels a sharp or flat and returns the note to its plain letter name. These three signs are called accidentals when they are written beside a note.

:::def Semitone :: the smallest gap between two notes, for example from a white key to the black key next to it.
:::def Accidental :: a sharp, flat or natural written just before a note. It lasts until the end of the bar.

An accidental is written to the left of the note it changes, at the same height, and it applies to every note on that line or space for the rest of the bar. The bar line cancels it. Tap the example to hear each change.

:::example accidentals

Some pairs of white keys have no black key between them. E to F and B to C are already a semitone apart. So E♯ is the same key as F, and C♭ is the same key as B. These spellings are rare in the music in this course, but you should know they exist.

## Staff and keyboard explorer

Place notes on the staff and see where they fall on the keyboard, or tap keys and see them on the staff. Try middle C, then find the same letter name an octave higher.
