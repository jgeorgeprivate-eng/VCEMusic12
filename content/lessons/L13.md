Notes and rhythms say what to play. Performance markings say how to play it: how loud, how smoothly, how fast and in what spirit. Most of them come from Italian, because Italian composers and printers set the conventions in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. These markings are where a performer's interpretation begins, and they are the terms you use when you describe your own playing.

:::def Performance markings :: the signs and words in a score that tell the performer how loud, how connected, how fast and with what character to play.

## Dynamics

Dynamics are the levels of loudness. They are written as letters under the staff, from pp (very soft) to ff (very loud). Tap each level and then the staff to hear the same melody at that level.

:::def Dynamics :: how loud or soft the music is. From soft to loud: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff.
:::def Piano (p) :: soft. Pianissimo (pp) is very soft, mezzo piano (mp) is moderately soft.
:::def Forte (f) :: loud. Fortissimo (ff) is very loud, mezzo forte (mf) is moderately loud.

:::example dynamics

Changes of loudness have their own signs. A crescendo, written cresc. or as a hairpin that opens to the right, means gradually louder. A diminuendo or decrescendo, written dim., decresc. or a hairpin that closes to the right, means gradually softer. A sudden accent on one note is marked sfz, sforzando.

:::def Crescendo :: gradually getting louder, shown by cresc. or an opening hairpin.
:::def Diminuendo :: gradually getting softer, shown by dim., decresc. or a closing hairpin.

:::example hairpin

## Articulation

Articulation is how each note starts, how it ends and how it connects to its neighbours. On the oboe it is mainly a matter of tonguing and air. The five markings in the course each have a sign on the note. Tap each one, then tap the staff to hear the same phrase played that way.

:::def Articulation :: the way notes are attacked, held and separated: detached, smooth, accented and so on.
:::def Staccato :: short and detached, shown by a dot above or below the note head.
:::def Legato :: smooth and connected, shown by a curved slur over the group of notes.
:::def Accent :: a note played with extra force, shown by a small wedge, >, above or below the note.
:::def Tenuto :: held for its full value and slightly stressed, shown by a short horizontal line on the note.
:::def Marcato :: strongly marked and separated, shown by a small upright wedge, ^, above the note.

:::example articulations

Legato is written with a slur. Remember that a slur joins notes of different pitches; a tie joins two notes of the same pitch into one. The slur also tells a wind player to play the notes in one breath without tonguing between them.

## Tempo

Tempo is the speed of the beat. It is given at the start of a piece, either as a metronome mark, such as ♩ = 120, meaning 120 crotchet beats per minute, or as an Italian word. The words give a range rather than an exact speed. Tap each term, then the staff, to hear the same tune at that speed.

:::def Tempo :: the speed of the music, measured in beats per minute or described with an Italian term.

:::example tempo

The main terms from slow to fast are largo, adagio, andante, moderato, allegro, vivace and presto. Changes of tempo are marked with accelerando (accel.), gradually faster; ritardando (rit.) or rallentando (rall.), gradually slower; and a tempo, back to the original speed. Rubato means the performer may stretch and compress the tempo freely for expression.

## Expression

Expression terms describe the character the composer wants. They are often added to a tempo word: Allegro con brio is fast with spirit; Andante cantabile is at a walking pace, in a singing style. The ones you will meet most are dolce (sweetly), cantabile (singing), espressivo (expressively), con brio (with spirit), maestoso (majestically), grazioso (gracefully), agitato (restless) and tranquillo (calm).

:::example melody tune=odeToJoy tempo="Allegro con brio" caption="Beethoven marked the Ode to Joy theme Allegro assai, very fast. Tap to hear it at an allegro tempo." voice=oboe

## Using markings in your own playing

When you prepare a piece, mark the score with what you intend: where the phrase peaks, where you breathe, which notes are tongued and which are slurred, and where the dynamics change. When you describe a performance, use these terms. "I played the second phrase legato and built a crescendo to the forte in bar 8" says exactly what you did.

:::aside Quick check :: The most common mistakes are mixing up a tie with a slur, and reading mp as louder than mf. Mezzo means moderately, so mp is a little louder than p and mf is a little softer than f. From soft to loud the order is pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff.

:::quiz
