Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds and silences in music. In staff notation, the shape of a note tells you how long it lasts, and a rest tells you how long to stay silent. The length of a note is measured in beats.

:::def Beat :: the steady pulse you tap your foot to. Note lengths are counted in beats.
:::def Rhythm :: the pattern of sound lengths and silences, measured against the beat.
:::def Rest :: a sign for silence. Each note value has a matching rest of the same length.

## The note values

Each note value is half the length of the one before it. A semibreve is a hollow oval with no stem. A minim is a hollow oval with a stem. A crotchet is a filled oval with a stem. A quaver adds a tail, or flag, to the stem. A semiquaver has two tails. When two or more quavers or semiquavers sit next to each other, their tails are usually joined into straight beams.

:::def Semibreve :: the longest common note, worth four crotchet beats.
:::def Minim :: worth two crotchet beats. Two minims fill a semibreve.
:::def Crotchet :: worth one beat in the metres used in this course. Four crotchets fill a semibreve.
:::def Quaver :: worth half a crotchet beat. Two quavers fill a crotchet.
:::def Semiquaver :: worth a quarter of a crotchet beat. Four semiquavers fill a crotchet.

:::example note-values

The names come from an old system where the semibreve was a short note. Some countries use fraction names instead: whole note, half note, quarter note, eighth note and sixteenth note. Both sets of names mean the same lengths.

## Dots and ties

A dot after a note makes it half as long again. A dotted minim lasts three beats, not two. A dotted crotchet lasts one and a half beats. A dotted quaver lasts three quarters of a beat, which is a quaver plus a semiquaver.

:::def Dotted note :: a note with a small dot after it. The dot adds half the note's value to its length.

:::example rhythm notes="h. q | q. e h | q e. s h" caption="A dotted minim and a crotchet, then a dotted crotchet, a quaver and a minim, then a crotchet, a dotted quaver, a semiquaver and a minim." count

A tie is a curved line joining two notes of the same pitch. The second note is not played again. Instead the first note is held for the length of both. Ties are used to carry a note across a bar line, or to write a length that no single note value gives.

:::def Tie :: a curved line between two notes of the same pitch, joining them into one longer sound.

:::example melody notes="G4h G4q G4q~ | G4q G4h. " key=C time=4/4 caption="The tie joins the last crotchet of bar 1 to the first crotchet of bar 2, so that note lasts two beats across the bar line." endbar=yes

A tie looks like a slur, but a slur joins notes of different pitches and means play them smoothly. If the two notes are the same pitch and joined by a curve, it is a tie.

## Rests

Every note value has a rest of the same length. The semibreve rest hangs below the fourth line. The minim rest sits on top of the middle line. The crotchet rest is a zigzag shape. The quaver rest has one hook and the semiquaver rest has two. A dot after a rest works the same way as a dot after a note.

:::example rhythm notes="q rq q rq | e re e re h | rh q q" caption="Crotchets with crotchet rests, quavers with quaver rests, then a minim rest." count

## Rhythm grids

A rhythm grid is another way to write rhythm. A bar is split into equal boxes, usually one box for each semiquaver, so four boxes to a beat. A mark in a box means a note starts there. Rhythm grids are common in drum programming and in classroom work. The same rhythm can always be written in staff notation.

## Rhythm grid tool

Build a rhythm in the grid and see it written on the staff. Tap Play to hear it against a click on each beat. Try to make each of these: four crotchets, a crotchet and two quavers, a dotted crotchet and a quaver, and four semiquavers.

:::tool rhythm-grid

:::aside Quick check :: To count a rhythm, say the beat numbers out loud and fit the notes to them. In 4/4, a dotted crotchet followed by a quaver is counted "1 and 2 and": the dotted crotchet lasts through "1 and 2", and the quaver falls on "and".

:::quiz
