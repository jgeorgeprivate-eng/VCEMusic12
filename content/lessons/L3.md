When you listen to music, some beats feel stronger than others. They come in regular groups: in a march the beats go strong, weak, strong, weak, and in a waltz they go strong, weak, weak. This grouping of beats is the metre. Written music shows it with bar lines and a time signature.

:::def Metre :: the regular grouping of beats into strong and weak, for example in twos, threes or fours.
:::def Bar :: one group of beats. Bar lines are the vertical lines across the staff that separate bars. The first beat of each bar is the strongest.
:::def Time signature :: the two numbers at the start of the music. The top number is how many beats in each bar. The bottom number is the note value that gets one beat: 4 means a crotchet, 8 means a quaver.

## 4/4 and 3/4

In 4/4 there are four crotchet beats in every bar. Beat 1 is the strongest, beat 3 is next strongest, and beats 2 and 4 are weak. Most pop, rock and marching music is in 4/4, which is why it is also called common time and is sometimes written as a C.

:::example metre time=4/4 caption="4/4: four crotchet beats in a bar. Tap to hear the accents."

In 3/4 there are three crotchet beats in every bar, with the first beat strongest and the other two weak. 3/4 is the metre of the waltz and of many slow songs.

:::example metre time=3/4 caption="3/4: three crotchet beats in a bar, strong weak weak."

The Blue Danube by Johann Strauss II is the best-known waltz there is. Count one two three along with it and feel how each bar leans on the first beat.

:::clip L3_Strauss_BlueDanube :: Johann Strauss II, The Blue Danube :: Count 1 2 3 with the beat. Notice how the oom-pah-pah accompaniment puts the bass note on beat 1 and the chords on beats 2 and 3.

## Filling a bar

The note values in a bar must add up to the number of beats in the time signature. In 4/4 a bar can hold a semibreve, or two minims, or four crotchets, or eight quavers, or any mix that adds to four beats. Rests count toward the total as well.

:::example rhythm notes="w | h h | q q q q | e e e e e e e e | h q e e" time=4/4 caption="Five ways to fill a bar of 4/4. Each bar adds up to four beats." count

:::example rhythm notes="h. | q q q | h q | q e e q" time=3/4 caption="Four ways to fill a bar of 3/4. Each bar adds up to three beats." count

## The rhythmic groupings

Rhythms in this course are built from a set of one-beat patterns. Each pattern fills one crotchet beat exactly, so you can combine them in any order to make a bar. Learn to recognise them by eye and by ear. Tap each one to hear it against the beat.

:::example groupings

Three longer values also appear: the minim (two beats), the dotted minim (three beats) and the semibreve (four beats), each of which fills more than one beat on its own. Together with a single quaver and a single crotchet, these are all the note groupings used in Unit 1.

The quaver triplet needs a word of its own. Three triplet quavers fit into one beat, where two ordinary quavers would. The small 3 above the group tells you to squeeze three notes evenly into the time of two.

:::def Triplet :: three notes played evenly in the time normally taken by two of the same value. It is marked with a small 3.

## Reading and writing rhythms

To read a rhythm, first find the beat from the time signature, then count the beats out loud and fit each note to its place. To write a rhythm you hear, tap the beat, work out where each note starts, and choose the grouping that matches each beat. The rhythm grid tool below lets you do this box by box and see the result in notation.

:::tool rhythm-grid

:::unit2 Unit 2 adds 6/8

In 6/8 the bottom number is 8, so the quaver is the counting unit and there are six quavers in a bar. But 6/8 is not felt as six beats. The quavers group in threes, so each bar has two main beats, each worth a dotted crotchet. Count it "one two three four five six" with the stress on one and four, or simply "one and a, two and a".

:::def Compound metre :: a metre where each main beat divides into three, as in 6/8. In simple metres like 4/4 and 3/4, each beat divides into two.

:::example metre time=6/8 caption="6/8: six quavers in a bar, felt as two beats of three."

Morning Mood from Grieg's Peer Gynt is in 6/8. Its flowing melody rises and falls in groups of three, two groups to a bar.

:::clip L3_Grieg_MorningMood :: Grieg, Morning Mood from Peer Gynt :: Count 1 2 3 4 5 6 in each bar with the stress on 1 and 4. Then count just 1 2 and feel the two-beat swing.

Three new groupings belong to 6/8. Each fills one dotted crotchet beat.

:::aside Quick check :: To tell 3/4 from 6/8 by ear, count the quick notes. If they group in twos under each strong beat and the strong beats come in threes, it is 3/4. If they group in threes under each strong beat and the strong beats come in twos, it is 6/8.
