A scale is a set of notes arranged in order from low to high. Most of the music you play is built from one scale at a time, so knowing the pattern of a scale tells you which notes to expect, which notes will sound settled and which will sound unfinished.

:::def Scale :: a set of notes arranged in order of pitch, usually covering one octave.
:::def Octave :: the distance from one note to the next note above or below it with the same name. C to the next C is an octave. It is eight letter names, counting both ends.

## Tones and semitones

On a keyboard, the gap between any key and the very next key, black or white, is a semitone. It is the smallest gap used in most Western music. Two semitones make a tone.

:::def Semitone :: the smallest step between two notes, for example C to C♯, or E to F.
:::def Tone :: two semitones, for example C to D, or E to F♯.

Most white keys have a black key between them, so the gap between them is a tone. There is no black key between E and F or between B and C, so those two gaps are semitones. Tap the pairs below to hear the difference. A semitone sounds tight and close. A tone sounds like a clear step.

:::example tone-semitone

## The major scale pattern

Every major scale uses the same pattern of gaps between its eight notes: tone, tone, semitone, tone, tone, tone, semitone. Written short, that is T T S T T T S.

C major is the scale with no sharps or flats. It uses only the white keys from C to the next C. Because the only semitones on the white keys are E to F and B to C, the C major scale puts its two semitones exactly where the pattern needs them: between the third and fourth notes, and between the seventh and eighth. Tap the staff to hear the scale.

:::example scale key=C caption="C major, one octave. Tap to hear."

## Scale degrees and the tonic

The notes of a scale are numbered from 1 to 8, starting on the note the scale is named after. These numbers are called scale degrees. The first degree has its own name, the tonic. It is the home note of the scale. Music in C major tends to start and finish on C, and a melody that ends anywhere else usually sounds unfinished.

:::def Tonic :: the first note of a scale and the note the scale is named after. It is the home note that music in that key returns to.
:::def Scale degree :: the position of a note in its scale, counted from the tonic as 1.

:::example scale key=C degrees caption="The same scale numbered by scale degree."

Degree 8 is the tonic again, an octave higher. In music language, the seventh degree is often called the leading note because it leads so strongly back up to the tonic. Play the first seven notes of the scale and stop. Your ear waits for the eighth.

:::example leading-note key=C caption="Seven notes, then a pause, then the tonic."

## Building a major scale on any note

You can build a major scale starting on any note by keeping to the pattern T T S T T T S. Two rules keep the spelling right. Use each letter name once, in order, so after G comes A, not G♯. Add a sharp or flat to any note that the pattern needs.

Build G major one gap at a time. Tap Start, then Next gap. Each tap measures the next gap, checks whether the plain letter name gives the right size, and adds a sharp if the gap would be too small.

:::example scale-steps key=G

Now build F major the same way. This time one gap comes out too wide, so a note has to be lowered with a flat.

:::example scale-steps key=F

G major has one sharp, F♯. F major has one flat, B♭. Every other major scale is built the same way, and the sharps or flats it needs are its key signature.

## Key signatures

Writing a sharp in front of every F in a piece in G major would be slow to read. Instead, the sharps or flats a key needs are written once at the start of each line, just after the clef. This group of sharps or flats is the key signature. A sharp in the key signature applies to every note with that letter name, in every octave, until a natural sign cancels it.

:::def Key signature :: the sharps or flats written at the start of each line of music. They apply to every note of that letter name, in any octave.
:::def Key :: the scale a piece of music is built on. A piece in G major is built from the notes of the G major scale.

Each major scale has its own key signature, so a key signature tells you the key of a piece. C major has no sharps or flats. G major has one sharp. F major has one flat. Here is G major written with its key signature instead of a sharp on each F♯.

:::example scale key=G keysig caption="G major with the key signature. The F♯ is in the key signature, so it is not written beside the note."

:::example scale key=F keysig caption="F major with the key signature. Every B is B♭."

:::unit2 Unit 2 adds two more keys

Unit 2 adds the major keys with two sharps or two flats. D major has F♯ and C♯. B♭ major has B♭ and E♭. Sharps are always added in the same order, F then C, and flats in the same order, B then E. That means a key signature of two sharps is always F♯ and C♯, never a different pair.

:::example scale key=D keysig caption="D major: F♯ and C♯."

:::example scale key=B♭ keysig caption="B♭ major: B♭ and E♭."

A quick way to name a major key from its key signature: with sharps, the tonic is one semitone above the last sharp, so F♯ gives G major and C♯ gives D major. With flats, the tonic is the second last flat, so B♭ and E♭ give B♭ major. F major has only one flat, so you simply learn it.

:::endunit2

## Hearing a major scale

A major scale has a bright, open sound. When you hear a scale played up or down, listen for two things. The steps are mostly tones, with the two semitones sitting at the third to fourth notes and the seventh to eighth. The last note sounds finished and settled. Later lessons compare this with the natural minor scale and the pentatonic scales, which place their gaps differently. Tap the scale to hear it on your own instrument's sound.

:::example scale key=C voice=oboe caption="C major played on an oboe sound. Then try the scale builder on any key."

## Writing a scale

When you are asked to write a major scale, work through these steps. Write the tonic. Write the next seven letter names in order, each on the next line or space. Apply the pattern T T S T T T S and add the sharps or flats the pattern needs. Check that your sharps or flats match the key signature of that key. Write the key signature at the start if the question asks for it, and then leave the accidentals off the notes.

:::aside Quick check :: In any major scale the semitones sit between degrees 3 and 4 and between degrees 7 and 8. If a scale you have written has a semitone anywhere else, a note needs a sharp or a flat.

:::quiz
