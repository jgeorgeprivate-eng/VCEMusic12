Major is not the only kind of scale. A great deal of music is in a minor key, which has a darker, sadder or more serious sound. The natural minor scale uses the same kind of building blocks as the major scale, tones and semitones, but arranges them in a different order.

:::def Minor scale :: a scale whose third note is a semitone lower than in the major scale starting on the same note. These lessons use the natural minor scale. There are other minor scales (harmonic and melodic) that raise some notes; you will meet them later.
:::def Natural minor :: the minor scale with the pattern tone, semitone, tone, tone, semitone, tone, tone. It uses exactly the notes of its relative major.

## The natural minor pattern

The pattern of the natural minor scale is T S T T S T T. The semitones come between degrees 2 and 3 and between degrees 5 and 6. Compare that with the major scale, where they come between 3 and 4 and between 7 and 8. Tap each scale to hear the difference. The minor scale sounds darker from its third note onward.

:::example compare-scales a=C b=Am la="C major: T T S T T T S" lb="A natural minor: T S T T S T T"

A minor is the natural minor scale with no sharps or flats. It uses the white keys from A to the next A.

## Relative minors

Every major key has a relative minor. The relative minor uses exactly the same notes and the same key signature as the major key, but starts on a different note: the sixth degree of the major scale. C major and A minor share the white keys, and A is the sixth note of C major. G major and E minor share one sharp, F♯. F major and D minor share one flat, B♭.

:::def Relative minor :: the minor key that shares a key signature with a major key. Its tonic is the sixth degree of the major scale, three semitones below the major tonic.
:::def Relative major :: the major key that shares a key signature with a minor key. Its tonic is the third degree of the minor scale.

:::example compare-scales a=G b=Em keysig la="G major, one sharp" lb="E minor, the same sharp"

:::example compare-scales a=F b=Dm keysig la="F major, one flat" lb="D minor, the same flat"

To find the relative minor of a major key, count down three semitones from the major tonic, or go up to degree 6. To find the relative major of a minor key, count up three semitones, or go up to degree 3. The key signature stays the same either way.

## Hearing major and minor

Because the two scales share notes, you cannot tell a piece's key from the key signature alone. The music itself tells you. Listen for the note that feels like home, the tonic. Then listen to the third note above it. If that third note is a tone and a half above the tonic, as in A to C, the music is minor. If it is two tones above, as in C to E, the music is major.

:::example compare-scales a=Am b=Dm la="A minor" lb="D minor" caption="Two minor scales. Each has the same dark colour because the pattern of gaps is the same."
