A pentatonic scale has five notes in an octave instead of seven. Pentatonic scales are found in folk music all over the world, in blues and rock, and in many well-known melodies. They are easy to sing and have no semitones, so any two notes sound comfortable together.

:::def Pentatonic scale :: a scale with five different notes in each octave. The name comes from the Greek word for five.

## The major pentatonic scale

The major pentatonic scale is a major scale with degrees 4 and 7 left out. In C, those are F and B, so C major pentatonic is C D E G A. Leaving out those two notes removes both semitones from the scale. The gaps are tone, tone, tone and a half, tone, tone and a half.

:::example pentatonic type=majorPentatonic tonic=C4 caption="C major pentatonic: C D E G A C. Degrees 4 and 7 of C major are left out."

Many familiar tunes use only the five notes of a major pentatonic scale. Amazing Grace is one. Tap it to hear it played slowly in 3/4, with each note lighting up as it sounds. Every note belongs to F major pentatonic: F G A C D.

:::example melody tune=amazingGrace voice=oboe caption="Amazing Grace. The notes F G A C D are F major pentatonic."

Auld Lang Syne uses the same five notes.

:::example melody tune=auldLangSyne caption="Auld Lang Syne, also in F major pentatonic."

## The minor pentatonic scale

The minor pentatonic scale is a natural minor scale with degrees 2 and 6 left out. A minor pentatonic is A C D E G. It contains the same five notes as C major pentatonic, just as A minor contains the same notes as C major. Its gaps are tone and a half, tone, tone, tone and a half, tone.

:::example pentatonic type=minorPentatonic tonic=A3 caption="A minor pentatonic: A C D E G A. Degrees 2 and 6 of A natural minor are left out."

The minor pentatonic scale is the basis of blues and rock guitar solos. Starting on the sixth degree of any major pentatonic scale gives its relative minor pentatonic.

## Hearing a pentatonic scale

A pentatonic scale has no semitones, so it has none of the pull of a leading note toward the tonic. Melodies built from it sound open and smooth. When you hear a scale and cannot find a semitone anywhere in it, it is probably pentatonic. Count the notes: five different notes to the octave rather than seven.
