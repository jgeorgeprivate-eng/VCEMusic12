A melody is a line of single notes, one after another, that you can sing. Before you can write a melody down or describe it, it helps to hear its shape. The shape a melody makes as it rises and falls is its contour.

:::def Melody :: a sequence of single pitches heard as one line. The tune.
:::def Contour :: the overall shape of a melody: where it rises, where it falls, where it stays level.

## Steps, leaps and repeated notes

The smallest building blocks of contour are the moves between one note and the next. A step moves to the next letter name up or down, a 2nd. A leap jumps over one or more letter names, a 3rd or more. A repeated note stays on the same pitch. Melodies that move mostly by step are smooth and easy to sing. Leaps add energy and stand out.

:::def Step :: a move from one note to the next letter name up or down, an interval of a 2nd. Also called conjunct motion.
:::def Leap :: a move that skips one or more letter names, an interval of a 3rd or larger. Also called disjunct motion.

:::example melody notes="C4q D4q E4q F4q | G4q E4q C4q G4q | G4q G4q A4q B4q | C5w" key=C time=4/4 contour caption="Bar 1 moves by step. Bar 2 leaps down a 3rd, a 3rd and up a 5th. Bar 3 repeats G then steps up. The dashed line traces the contour." endbar=yes

## Shapes

Six words describe most contours. Rising: the melody climbs overall. Falling: it descends overall. Arch: it rises then falls, like a hill. Inverted arch: it falls then rises, like a valley. Repeated: it stays on or near one note. Wave: it rises and falls several times. Tap each shape to see it drawn on the staff, then tap the staff to hear it.

:::example contours

Real melodies often combine shapes. A phrase might rise by step to a high point and then fall by leap. When you describe a melody, say which shape fits the whole phrase, then mention the moves that stand out, such as a wide leap or a long run of steps.

## Range and register

Two other words go with contour. Range is the distance between the lowest and highest notes of a melody. A melody with a range of a 5th stays close; a range of more than an octave is wide. Register is where the melody sits overall: high, middle or low for the instrument or voice. A melody can have a small range in a high register, like a piccolo part, or a wide range in a low register.

:::def Range :: the distance from the lowest to the highest note of a melody or a part.
:::def Register :: how high or low a melody sits in the overall span of an instrument or voice.

:::example melody notes="G4e A4e B4e C5e D5q E5q | F5q E5q D5q C5q | B4q A4q G4h" key=C time=4/4 contour caption="An arch with a range of a 7th, from G4 up to F5 and back." endbar=yes

## Hearing contour

When you listen to a melody, trace its shape in the air with your hand. Notice where it reaches its highest note, which is usually the point of most tension, and where it comes to rest. Then ask whether it moved by steps or leaps to get there. These two observations, the shape and the kind of motion, are the beginning of melodic dictation, which the next lesson covers.

:::example melody tune=odeToJoy contour caption="The opening of Ode to Joy: a small range, almost all steps, a gentle arch." voice=oboe

:::aside Quick check :: In a listening question about melody, name the contour, the kind of motion (step or leap) and the range. Three precise words say more than a paragraph.

:::quiz
