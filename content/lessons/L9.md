Melodic dictation means hearing a short melody and writing it down in notation. It brings together everything from the earlier lessons: the major scale, scale degrees, intervals and contour. In these lessons the melodies are short, up to four beats, and use the notes of a major scale.

:::def Dictation :: writing down music from hearing it rather than reading it.

## The method

A good order to work in is this. First, find the tonic. The app or your teacher will play the tonic or the scale before the melody. Sing it in your head and keep it there. Second, listen to the whole melody once without writing anything, and notice its contour: where it rises, falls and rests. Third, identify the first note. In these lessons the melodies usually start on the tonic, the third or the fifth. Fourth, work out each move from note to note. Is it a repeat, a step up or down, or a leap? For a leap, work out the interval or hear which scale degree it lands on. Fifth, write the notes on the staff in the key given, and check the rhythm fits the beats.

## Using scale degrees

The quickest way to hear a melody is as scale degrees rather than as intervals. Sing the major scale up and down with the numbers 1 to 8 until each number has its own feel. Degree 1 is home. Degree 5 is strong and stable. Degree 7 wants to rise to 8. Degree 4 often falls to 3. When you hear a note, ask which degree it is, then write the letter name that degree has in the key.

## Common patterns

Certain moves come up again and again. A step up and back: 1 2 1. A rising scale: 1 2 3 4. Outlining the tonic triad: 1 3 5 or 5 3 1. A leap to 5 then steps down: 1 5 4 3. Learning to recognise these small patterns means you write a melody as two or three chunks rather than as separate notes.

:::example melody notes="C4q E4q G4q E4q | C4q D4q E4q C4q | G4q F4q E4q D4q | C4w" key=C time=4/4 caption="Four one-bar patterns: 1 3 5 3, then 1 2 3 1, then 5 4 3 2, then 1." endbar=yes

## Dictation pad

Tap New melody. Listen as many times as you need, enter the four notes on the keyboard, and check. Start with steps only, then allow leaps. The melody always starts on the tonic and the key is shown.

:::tool dictation-pad

:::aside Quick check :: Write the rhythm first if the melody uses quavers, then fit the pitches. If a note is wrong, it is most often a step mistaken for a repeat, or a 3rd mistaken for a 2nd. Compare your version with the original note by note.

:::quiz
