Everything you have learned to hear in other people's music applies to the pieces on your own stand. Knowing the key, the scales and patterns, the structure and the elements of a piece you are preparing changes how you practise it: you know which scale to warm up with, where the sections begin, which phrase is the climax and what the composer wants the music to do. This lesson turns the listening template into a plan for each performance piece.

:::def Performance piece :: a work you are preparing to play to an audience, solo or in a group.

## Start with the key

Read the key signature and the first and last notes. Decide major or minor by the third degree in the melody and the chord the piece ends on. Then look for a change of key in the middle; the sign is accidentals that keep appearing, usually sharps raising the leading note of a new key. Write the key and any changes in the piece planner.

## Find the scales and patterns

Mark every scale run, broken chord and repeated figure in the music. Most passages that look hard are a scale or an arpeggio in disguise. Once you know that bar 12 is the D major scale in semiquavers, the warm-up that fixes bar 12 is obvious: the D major scale in semiquavers.

:::tool scale-chord-builder

## Mark the structure

Find the sections and write bar numbers for each. Look for repeat signs, double bar lines, changes of key or tempo, and the return of the opening. Give each section a letter, as in lesson A4. Knowing the plan tells you where to breathe, where to build and where to relax, and it is what you will say in the discussion of your pieces.

## Elements that matter most

Go through the seven elements for the piece as you would for a listening task, but with your own playing in mind. Duration: tempo and the rhythm patterns you will have to control. Pitch: range, the highest and lowest notes, leaps. Tone colour: what sound the piece asks for. Texture: your role, melody or accompaniment, solo or part of a group. Dynamics and articulation: the markings, and the places where none are written and you must decide.

## Piece planner

Each piece you are preparing has a plan in the practice room: key and tonality, scales and patterns, structure, the elements that matter most, the technical and expressive challenges, the intended effect and the exercises you have designed. Fill in the first four parts now; the next lessons fill in the rest.

:::aside Quick check :: If you cannot name the key, the scale behind the hardest passage and the sections of a piece, you do not yet know it well enough to practise it efficiently. Ten minutes with the score and the planner saves hours.

:::quiz
