Every piece has passages that are hard to play and passages that are hard to play well. The first are technical challenges: fingers, breath, range, speed, tuning. The second are expressive challenges: phrasing, dynamics, tone, character. Naming them precisely is the first step to solving them, and it is what you will be asked to discuss about your performances.

:::def Technical challenge :: a physical difficulty in playing the notes: fingering, breath control, range, fast passages, tuning, rhythm.
:::def Expressive challenge :: a difficulty in making the music sound as intended: shaping phrases, controlling dynamics, matching tone to character, sustaining a line.

## Technical challenges on the oboe

Breath: long phrases, and phrases with nowhere to breathe. Range: the top register where the tone thins, and the bottom notes that speak late. Fingering: passages across the break, fast runs, awkward combinations such as F to E♭. Tuning: the notes that are naturally sharp or flat on your instrument, and sustained notes in unison with others. Rhythm: syncopation, triplets against duplets, counting rests. Reeds: a reed that suits the piece's range and dynamics.

## Expressive challenges

Phrasing: where each phrase begins and ends, and where its high point is. Dynamics: a true pianissimo that still speaks; a forte that stays in tune; a crescendo spread evenly over four bars. Tone: a warm, even sound across the range; a change of colour between sections. Character: playing a dance so it dances, a lament so it mourns. Endings: finishing a note cleanly and leaving silence.

## Finding them

Play the piece through once, slowly, and mark every place you stumble or feel unsure. Then play it at tempo and mark where it falls apart. The marked places are your technical challenges. Record the run-through and listen as if it were someone else's playing, with the listening template: where does the phrasing flatten, where do the dynamics stay the same, where does the tone change for the worse? Those are your expressive challenges.

## Planning the fix

For each challenge, write one sentence: what it is, where it is (bar numbers), and what you will do about it. "Bar 12 to 14: semiquaver run across the break; practise at half speed in dotted rhythms, then build up." "Bars 20 to 27: one long phrase with no breath; plan a quick breath after beat 2 of bar 23 and practise the phrase from there." The piece planner has a field for each kind of challenge; lesson P3 covers designing the exercises.

## Discussing your pieces

When you discuss the challenges in a performance, use the same structure: the challenge, its place in the music, the practice approach, and what happened. Musical terms and bar numbers make the discussion specific. "The expressive challenge in the slow movement was sustaining the line through the long notes in bars 5 to 8; I practised them with the drone and worked on breath support, and in the performance the line held but the top A was slightly sharp."

:::aside Quick check :: A challenge written as "the fast bit" is not yet a plan. A challenge written as "bars 12 to 14, semiquavers across the break" is.

:::quiz
