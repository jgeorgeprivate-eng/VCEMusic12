Practice works when it has a goal and a record. A goal tells you what to do in the next twenty minutes; a record tells you what you did and whether it worked. The practice journal in this app takes a minute after each session and earns a life once a day. Over a term it becomes the evidence for the discussion of your approach and the reflection on feedback.

:::def Practice goal :: one specific thing to achieve in a session, such as bars 12 to 14 at 80 beats a minute without a stumble.

## A session with a shape

Warm up with the exercises from lesson P3, five minutes. Work on the goal, ten to fifteen minutes: slow, in loops, with the metronome, building up. Play through something you can already play, so the session ends with music rather than drill. Then write the journal entry.

## Setting goals

Goals come from the challenges in the piece planner. Make them small and measurable: a tempo, a number of clean repetitions, a passage played from memory. "Get better at the piece" is not a goal. "Bars 20 to 27 in one breath, three times in a row" is.

## The journal

After each session, write four things: what you worked on, what went well, one thing to improve, and the next goal. Add the minutes. Keep each line short. The point is a record you will read back, not an essay. After a few weeks, read the entries for one piece in order and notice what changed.

## Using feedback

When your teacher or an audience gives feedback, write it in the journal in their words, then write what you will do about it. Reflecting on feedback means more than agreeing with it: it means turning it into a goal, trying the goal, and reporting what happened. That loop, feedback to goal to result, is what you describe when you discuss your approach to a performance.

## Practice journal

The journal is in the practice room. Write today's entry after your next session.

:::aside Quick check :: If you cannot say what the goal of today's session was in one sentence, set tomorrow's goal before you start.

:::quiz
