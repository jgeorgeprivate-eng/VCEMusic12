At some point you will be asked to perform a piece so that it conveys a particular effect, and to describe how you intend to achieve it. The notes are given; the effect is made by your choices: tempo, dynamics, articulation, tone, phrasing and timing. Lesson A6 showed how composers create effect; this lesson is about doing it as a performer.

:::def Interpretation :: the performer's choices about how to play the written notes to convey a character or effect.

## Decide the effect

Say in one or two words what the piece, or the passage, should convey: calm, grief, playfulness, grandeur, menace, tenderness, excitement. Look for what the composer tells you: the tempo word, the expression terms, the dynamics, the title. Then decide what you will add in the places where nothing is written.

## The performer's choices

Tempo: within the marking, faster is lighter and more urgent; slower is heavier and more serious. Dynamics: the overall level, how wide the range is, and where the single loudest and softest moments fall. Articulation: how short the staccato, how connected the legato, how hard the accents. Tone: bright or dark, with more or less vibrato, which on the oboe is a large part of the colour. Phrasing: where each phrase breathes and where it peaks. Timing: a little stretching at the top of a phrase, a slight hold before an important note, a steady pulse where drive is wanted.

## Try it on your own piece

Take one phrase and play it three ways, each for a different effect, changing only what you choose. Record each version in the practice room and listen back. Decide which choices made the difference, and write them in the piece planner under Intended effect: "Tender: slower than marked, piano with a swell to mezzo forte in bar 3, legato throughout, warm tone with light vibrato, a slight hold on the top note."

## Describing it

Your teacher will want the description as well as the performance. Write it as effect, then choices, then where: "The effect is calm. I will take the tempo at the slower end of andante, keep the dynamics between piano and mezzo piano, slur every phrase, and use a warm tone with gentle vibrato. The one swell comes at bar 9, the high point of the piece, and I will let the last note fade rather than cut it off."

## Your recording against a professional one

Lesson A7 asked you to compare your recording of a piece with a professional one. Do it for the effect piece. The question is not who plays better, but which choices the professional made for the effect, and whether you want to borrow them.

:::aside Quick check :: For each effect you want, name at least three choices you will make, and the bar where the listener should notice each one.

:::quiz
